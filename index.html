A quick grammar refresher

States describe possession, opinions, feelings, or perception: “I own a car”; “I know the answer.” They usually use simple forms, not continuous ones.

Actions can use continuous forms: “I’m having lunch”; “The chef is tasting the soup.” The meaning matters: “I think” expresses an opinion, while “I’m thinking” describes considering something.

Usage note: These activities follow the worksheet’s answer key. In modern informal English, expressions such as “I’m really liking…” can be acceptable for a developing or temporary feeling. Perception verbs can also have different meanings in different contexts.
